# Étienne de Crécy: Beats 'n' Cubes (research notes)

Reference video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zjs24FUrprg ("Etienne de Crecy - Welcome"). The page text gives only that title. Four public thumbnail frames show the cube from above with a cyan-white wireframe grid on its walls, a flat red panel layout with a silhouette in the center, and two tilted views (white grid, red).

Facts below come from the sources listed at the end. Where sources disagree, both are noted. Nothing here is from watching the video's full length.

## What it is

- A live electronic show: de Crécy plays alone, inside the middle of a large cube, with projections mapped onto it. The cube *is* the screen.
- It premiered in 2007 (TransMusicales, Rennes) and toured for several years; sources say 5 years or nearly 7 (they disagree). He says he changed the show three times (music and visuals) over the tour.
- Motivation (Philharmonie de Paris article): after Daft Punk's 2006 pyramid tour he asked François Wunschel and Pier Schneider (EXYZT, now 1024 Architecture) to build something to compete. He also said three people "just pushing buttons" was not enough of a stage show.
- The stage identity: he is "not a front man"; he wants the audience to look at the cube and see a machine show. He refused to play one venue where the cube did not fit.

## The structure

- Name: the Square Cube. About 6 m x 6 m (one source says 20 ft). Scaffolding.
- A 3x3 grid: nine blocks / a "3x3 tower of light-boxes".
- Covered in translucent fabric: three layers of fabric and tubes stretched over the scaffolding. The layering gives depth, so the projections read as three-dimensional.
- One projector (per an AntiVJ excerpt on Visual Music Archive).
- No conventional stage lights (de Crécy, Philharmonie interview). Some "gear within the cubes" as well as projection (Cargo Collective interview).
- Built on site; quick to install.

## The visuals

- Brief he gave: "8-bit, square and simple". The 1024 Architecture subtitle also says 8-bit.
- Reported vocabulary: solid lines of choreographed geometry, optical illusions, gothic archways, Rubik's-cube-like rotating shapes, 3D models mapped onto the structure.
- Black and white first, then colour (Philharmonie interview).
- Needs a very dark room (Coachella 2008 slot was moved to last in the tent for this).
- Visual design was joint work with EXYZT. No named visual artist or VJ for the original cube was found.

## How it is driven

- "The lights rhythmically follow the music that I play." He triggers lights and visual sequences from inside the cube.
- Fully live, no laptop. Gear named on Visual Music Archive and in a Skiddle listing: Akai MPC1000, two Roland TB-303, Novation Bass Station, mixer.
- 1024 Architecture says the images are synchronised to the music through an interface designed for the show (the page gives "EVS" in brackets without explaining it). The page tags include DMX.
- No source says whether sync is timecode, MIDI, audio analysis or manual cues.
- Visual Music Archive uses the track "Welcome" as an example of how he builds tracks on stage (a siren and an acid stomp).

## Not found

- Any phases, acts, sections, set list or timeline of the show. No source describes the order of the visuals or the arc of the set.
- Colour palette beyond "black and white, then colour".
- What the inside faces show versus the outside.
- Software for the original cube.

The only way to get the phases is to watch the video (or other recordings, e.g. Rock en Seine 2011: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hXafl0-DnvU) and note what changes and when.

## Later evolution: Space Echo (2019 onward)

- A different show. Staging by Alexandre Lebrun (LightLab), artistic direction and video by Armand Béraud.
- 13 transparent LED screens (70% transparent) arranged in an equilateral triangle, each on a motor so the arrangement can reconfigure during the show. Transparency lets images have real depth; content runs from strict geometry to blurry masses.
- Software: Smode (live image generation and mixing, parameters linkable to input controllers). Team and credits are in the Smode case study.

## What this suggests for our three-screen version

- The 3x3 block grid and translucent depth layers map naturally onto our three panels (and the handoff's later 3x3 option).
- The look is edge-lit lines and subdividing boxes, in a limited palette, switching as the set progresses. It reads as architecture being animated, not a scene.
- A single "phase" model (what the cube looks like in each part of the set) is the missing piece, and it has to come from the video.
